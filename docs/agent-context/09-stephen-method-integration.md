# Stephen: research method and integration owner

## What stays with you

Define the new capability, choose fair comparisons, freeze the evaluation and decide whether the result justifies a paper. Students can implement pieces of the selector after you specify it, but assigning each a platform does not transfer responsibility for the scientific argument.

Own the common interface/schema PR, integration branch, task split, reference/default definition, model/runtime choice, statistical plan and manuscript boundaries with the existing A-LEMS/platform/RAF work.

## First scientific prototype

Specify a small-budget selector using observables available before exhaustive evaluation: ready tool overlap, dependency critical path, observed CPU demand, model tokens/timing and measured initial-state indicators where reliable. Begin with an interpretable rule or small fitted model. Complexity is not evidence of novelty.

Before implementation, write down: input availability and cost; candidate eligibility; profiling budget; how uncertainty changes a recommendation; and the conditions for abstention. Compare at equal budget with uniform profiling and ordinary factorial screening. A method that secretly uses full reference profiles has not reduced profiling cost.

The main scientific uncertainty is whether workflow/state information improves decisions enough to matter. Four candidates may be easy for ordinary screening. The pilot must identify a real opportunity and mechanism; do not enlarge the search space merely to make a baseline expensive.

## Freeze before held-out results

Declare primary measured energy domains, goal boundary, failure/retry policy, preparation protocol, latency constraint and allowable completion degradation. Choose these from the service/task contract and development evidence, not after seeing favorable outcomes. Define how partial energy data will be reported.

Use paired comparisons and uncertainty at task/session level. Sensor samples are not independent trials. Repetitions characterize variability within goals; they do not turn 40 held-out goals into hundreds of independent tasks. Estimate precision from the pilot; three repetitions are a planning starting point, not a power analysis.

Measure both conditional effect and live benefit. Report energy per verified completion with success and latency, recommendation agreement/regret relative to the best tested feasible candidate with uncertainty, profiling time/energy and development cost. If deployment savings require amortizing profiling across repeated goals, show the break-even workload under explicit reuse assumptions.

## Required paper evidence

1. A relevant decision that time/token/phase accounting alone sometimes gets wrong.
2. Matched physical experiments explaining the mechanism and replay limits.
3. Held-out recommendation quality or reduced profiling cost beyond strong simple methods.
4. Live correctness, energy and latency under recommended changes.
5. Ablations, no-benefit cases, measurement scope/coverage and reproducible artifacts.

These are targets, not promised outcomes. One well-characterized primary environment supports a scoped empirical claim; a second platform should test a specified transfer hypothesis. More GPUs do not establish a better contribution.

## Integration decisions

Hold a short integration checkpoint whenever a component interface changes. Merge small PRs in dependency order, tag pilot and campaign versions, and keep one authoritative configuration. Changes to verifier, task assets, runtime, sensor scope or candidate semantics after a freeze require a new experiment version and an impact assessment.

If only two students are available, keep the runner/integration work with you. A fourth student can independently reproduce measurements and check exports after the shared pipeline exists.
